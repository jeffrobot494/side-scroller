1 · Top bar — readiness across the task force

Everything mocked at three players on purpose: a layout that works for three works for two, and one built for two does not grow. Nothing anywhere says "the other player" — it says who, by name.

The shared doom clock stays exactly where it is — one number in the bar, same as single player. The button changes from "Advance the day" to "Ready", because a day is no longer something you buy alone. Ready is a toggle — the button stays lit while you are ready and clicking it again stands you down, which also releases whatever deployment you had pending so you can change it. There is no window to stand down once you are the last to ready: the day turns on that click.

"Share with ▾" is a menu rather than a button because at three players you have to pick who — the two-player version would have been a single toggle that stops working the moment a third joins. A lead someone gave you is tagged with their name and is otherwise an ordinary lead: you can deploy to it, and you can pass it on. The finale shares the same control, which is what "the finale is disclosable like any lead" means. Not shown, deliberately: any count of the leads you cannot see.

3 · Deploy — the commitment

Locked on ready. When every commander has readied, this deployment is final — there is no withdrawing, and you will not know where anyone else went until it is over.

What the design already decided and the screen has to say out loud: choices lock and cannot be withdrawn. If two commanders happen to pick the same lead, they play it together; nothing on this screen promises they won't. Disclosure is not made here — sharing a lead is §2's control, on the lead itself.

"Elsewhere today" is the only thing you learn about anyone else, and it is one line per commander — which lead they took, or that they stayed home. Deliberately absent: whether they won, who died, and what they carried out. The sector number in the top bar will have moved whether or not they tell you why. This list grows a row per player, so it does not care how many there are.

Your squad keeps the position and treatment it has today; other squads stack beside it, one block per commander, each in that commander's colour so a soldier on screen reads as theirs at a glance. Only commanders on this level appear — Brazil went elsewhere and is not here. At four squads of three this is twelve bars, which is the practical ceiling this layout should be tested against.

6 · End of campaign — three outcomes, not two

★

SECTOR SECURED

You cleared the finale. The campaign ends.

☠

SECTOR LOST

The doom clock ran out. Everyone loses, together.

—

THE WAR ENDED WITHOUT YOU

Another commander finished it. You did not fail, and you did not win.

The third panel is new and has no equivalent today. It is what "victory is individual and defeat is collective" produces: one commander can end the campaign for everyone by winning it, and the rest get an ending that is neither of the two the game currently knows how to show.
