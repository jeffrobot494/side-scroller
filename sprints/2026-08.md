# August 2026

*Agents that know where they are*

**Aug 2 – Aug 31. ~15–20 hrs/week, most of it playtesting and judgment, not typing.**

## The shape

Right now nothing in the game reasons about space. An agent perceives three facts
about the level — whether a wall blocks its view, whether there is floor under its
front foot, and whether its target is above or below — and every behavior it has
is expressed relative to whatever it is fighting. This month that changes: agents
get a model of where they can stand, a way to choose a place to be, and a way to
get there. By August 31 an enemy that wants to reach you goes around, climbs up,
or gives up honestly, and a companion follows you across terrain instead of
pressing into it.

## How this sprint is scoped

**By decisions, not by work.** Implementation is fast; judgment is not. The
constraint is how many things you can actually watch, feel, and rule on in a
month — so the milestones below are moments of judgment, not moments of
completion.

The corollary, and the one rule that matters: **when a build lands early, spend
the remaining time playing it, not starting the next one.** Pulling work forward
is how you arrive at August 31 with everything built and nothing decided. That is
the failure mode for this sprint, not running out of time.

## Success

**The sprint succeeds if the four questions below have answers you trust** — not
if the code exists. Code that shipped and was never judged counts as unfinished.

**Fully delivered:** navigation runs in the Lab, across the whole enemy roster in
real missions, and for companions; and the review records what you learned.

## The four questions

Answerable only by watching. Record answers in the review as they resolve.

1. **Does a navigating enemy feel smarter — or just slower?** The real risk.
   Deliberate movement can read as hesitant, and hesitant is worse than dumb.
2. **Should an agent ever fail a jump?** Perfect traversal reads robotic; a missed
   ledge reads alive. Cheap to allow, hard to retrofit later.
3. **Should the player be able to break pursuit using terrain?** Large gameplay
   consequence; determines how memory and repathing work.
4. **Is a committed route readable?** If you can see where an enemy is heading you
   can outplay it. If it repaths continuously, you can't.

## Arc

Each week is gated by a judgment, not a build.

| Week | Built | What you decide |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (Aug 2–9) | Nav graph, spatial senses, travel, Lab v2 | Click a point — does the agent move *legibly*? Deliberate, or drunk? Q2 and Q4 start here |
| 2 (Aug 10–16) | Whole enemy roster on navigation | Play real missions. **Q1** — smarter or slower? This is the week that matters |
| 3 (Aug 17–23) | Companions navigate; tuning from weeks 1–2 | Does the squad feel competent or annoying? Q3 |
| 4 (Aug 24–31) | Tuning only | Answer all four. Write the review |

Week 1's build is probably a couple of sessions. The rest of week 1 is watching
it. That is not slack — it is the work.

- The explicit "jump now" locomotor instruction, replacing both current guesses
- Fallback to today's steering when off-graph or unreachable; stuck detector
- All six built-in enemy specs on navigation
- Companion escort by navigation

### Behavior Lab v2 — locked scope

Replaces `src/editor/tools/behavior-lab.js` entirely:

- a randomly generated level
- one agent
- click anywhere to set that point as its goal
- watch it get there
- the nav graph and current route as overlays

Nothing else. No combat, no teams, no rosters, no scoreboards, no levers. Being
quick to build is not a reason to add anything — every v1 feature returns later,
one at a time, each earning its place against a question it answers. Model on
`firing-room.js`; register per the usual recipe.

## Explicitly out

Out because they are more than you can *judge* this month, not because they are
too much work:

- **Destination scoring for combat** (`engage` weights, shot line from candidate
  nodes). A second large design surface with its own tuning loop. September.
  Missions this month have navigating movement and unchanged combat behavior —
  that combination is coherent and playable.
- Cover, dodging, group coordination, habit tracking, flyer terrain grinding.
- The design doc browser, category scheme, sprint tooling. Parking lot.
